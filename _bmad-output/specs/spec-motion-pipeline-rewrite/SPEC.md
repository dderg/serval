# Motion-pipeline rewrite — geometry-first planner

## Why

A vision to realize and an opportunity to capture. The current planner reparameterizes a cubic-Bézier path to arc length (a per-segment numerical table) and runs a convex SOCP plus an SLP jerk-refinement loop plus iterative junction joining — correct, but too heavy to keep up in real time, and it never refits the faceted G1 stream into continuous motion (it caps junction velocity at geometric kinks instead of inserting smooth blends). The rewrite keeps the proven MCU/execution and structured-logging layers and restructures the geometry-and-planning front: a typed segment library (`Line | Arc | Clothoid` + a follower channel), a shaper-agnostic fitter that turns corners into clothoid blends, and a velocity planner that decouples lateral handling (into the geometry) from tangential jerk (a 1D S-curve in lookahead) — which lets the heavy convex solver drop out in favor of a fast forward-backward sweep. The ultimate payoff is the project's non-negotiable, minimum print time, but the immediate work is the *foundation*: interfaces that let each piece be tuned to its own optimum later, with no layer capping what an adjacent piece can achieve. The foundation's job is to not get in the way. Affected: anyone running this fork on real hardware, where throughput is the whole point.

## Capabilities

- id: CAP-1
  intent: The planner can ingest slicer G-code (G0/G1/G2/G3) and produce an internal geometric path of typed segments for any printable move, with extrusion carried as a follower channel.
  success: For a representative slicer program, the reconstructed path stays within junction-deviation δ of the intended geometry at every point, and a pure retraction (`G1 E-` with no XYZ) plans as a zero-length-spatial-path follower move.

- id: CAP-2
  intent: The planner can compute a time-optimal speed profile for a given geometry under the machine's acceleration limits via a forward-backward sweep, not a convex solver.
  success: For test and recorded move sequences in the accel-only configuration, the speed profile is no slower than the current planner's under the same limits — checkable offline, without whole-print simulation.

- id: CAP-3
  intent: The planner can refit sharp corners and tight arc seams into curvature-continuous clothoid blends, raising cornering speed within δ.
  success: For a corner test case the fitter emits a clothoid blend that is curvature-continuous (G2) and collinear (G1) with its neighbors and stays within δ; cornering speed exceeds a full-stop junction, and the blend is set by δ and max_accel alone, independently of the velocity planner.

- id: CAP-4
  intent: The planner can emit position-vs-time for execution, composed from the geometry and the speed profile, evaluable at the MCU's fixed step rate.
  success: The fixed-rate evaluator reproduces the planned path within step tolerance at the configured frequency; clothoid position is produced by a Fresnel approximation, not a per-segment arc-length table.

- id: CAP-5
  intent: The planner can handle 3D corner blends and any position-dependent Z field (bed mesh) while keeping planning effort at 2D cost — the Z field is evaluated along the path parameter, never split into facets.
  success: A vase-mode print and a meshed print both plan with the mesh-induced Z folded in as a velocity/acceleration cap (`v_z = v·dZ/ds` from the mesh gradient along the path, with the along-path Z curvature feeding the 3D κ), not as a pre-faceted Z stream; a mesh too steep for the Z limit fails loudly rather than crawling silently.

- id: CAP-6
  intent: Input shaping and pressure advance run as per-axis time-domain post-processors at execution; the extruder follower advances on the post-shaper (realized) velocity.
  success: A pure retraction plans as a virtual-path follower move; the follower position is reconstructed from the shaped signal (odometer) so PA reflects actual filament speed; shaper and PA never run upstream of velocity planning.

- id: CAP-7
  intent: The front-end can gate moves inside a cancelled-object region (exclude_object) — suppressing their extrusion and routing travel — without perturbing the geometry or planning of the retained stream.
  success: Cancelling an object mid-print drops exactly its moves and the planner continues across the resume seam with no added artifact or stall; a never-excluded print plans identically to no exclude_object present.

- id: CAP-8
  intent: The planner can apply a slowly-varying global Z bias (z_thermal_adjust) at execution lowering, outside geometry and the velocity caps.
  success: A thermal-drift offset shifts all emitted Z by the current bias with planning, geometry, and caps unchanged; the bias updates between moves, never mid-move.

- id: CAP-9
  intent: (Deferred, post-V1) The planner can modulate a named parameter as a function of Z height during a calibration print (tuning_tower).
  success: A tuning-tower program ramps the target parameter with height at execution; until then, the execution layer leaves a clean seam to add it without touching the geometry or velocity layers.

## Constraints

- Minimum print time is the objective; smoothness is only a means to raise the acceleration ceiling, never an end. The planner never knowingly ships a cheaper algorithm that yields a measurably slower trajectory than the best computable on the active hardware.
- Fail loudly: a too-steep mesh, a late segment, an infeasible move, or a segment that cannot absorb its own jerk-smoothing raises an explicit error code — never a padded start time or a silent slowdown.
- Design representation (curvature, via typed segments) and execution representation (time-polynomials) are separate artifacts; no single object serves both roles. Jerk-smoothing lives entirely in `s(t)` (timing) and never alters geometry — so it can produce no geometric artifact; its only costs are time and required braking length.
- The fitter is **shaper-agnostic by authority, not by approximation**: the planner never limits acceleration for ringing reasons. `max_accel` is the user's sovereign knob (resonance measurement is imperfect; `calibrate_shaper` only suggests). Clothoid smoothness helps the shaper one-way, never negotiated.
- Lateral (centripetal) jerk is bounded for free by the clothoid geometry wherever the fitter has made κ continuous — no jerk constraint for it in the planner there. Tangential jerk is a 1D S-curve in the velocity lookahead (not a post-processor); on short straights it trims peak velocity so the accel→decel reversal fits a bounded-jerk ramp.
- Velocity planning is a forward-backward sweep over closed-form clothoid corner caps with S-curve ramps — not a convex SOCP. Decoupling lateral (geometry) from tangential (1D) is what removes the heavy solver and buys real-time. The sweep is **node-based, not grid-based**: because clothoid κ is linear its peak is a closed-form endpoint and junction caps are closed-form, so the sweep visits a finite node set (one per junction + per clothoid apex) with analytic constant-accel kinematics between — there is **no arc-length planning grid** (the old 1024-point reparameterization table is deleted, not replaced) and no uniform discretization to "straddle an apex." The only residual numerical integration anywhere in the planner is the step-7 limit-riding `1D ODE per clothoid` (local, adaptive). Correctness rests on a **node-coverage invariant** — every junction/apex is a node, no κ-step lives mid-segment (fail-loud) — not on a grid resolution. *(Grounded: segment-IR contract research pass, 2026-06-18.)*
- The lookahead window is bounded by the **worst-case braking length**: the distance to decelerate from the velocity cap to a full stop under the tightest axis accel, padded for the jerk-limited S-curve ramp. A node earlier than that backward reach is invariant to anything downstream — it can be pinned and the suffix re-solved without changing it — so it is safe to commit; the freeze point snaps back to the nearest corner within reach. Moves past the committed boundary have been streamed to the MCU and are never re-planned. (Precedent: the current planner already bounds its sub-window this way — `bounded_freeze_idx`, `braking_time = k·v_cruise/a_brake`.)
- Acceleration is the invariant; corner velocity is derived (`v = √(a/κ)`). Each clothoid carries a pointwise cap `v ≤ √(a/κ(s))` (the apex binds); the velocity profile over a clothoid is an output of the global lookahead, never prescribed locally (it may be all-decel, all-accel, a dip, or cruise). The effective speed ceiling at any point is `min(F, max_velocity(d), √(a/κ(s)), v_flow)`, where `v_flow = Q_max/(w·h)` is the hotend volumetric-flow cap; where κ→0 (straights) the curvature term drops out and the remaining terms bind. The flow cap must be checked against the **pressure-advance-augmented transient** `(v + τ·a)`, not just `v` — coming out of a corner the PA push can transiently exceed `Q_max` while steady-state `v` is within bounds; never command an extrusion demand the hotend cannot melt. *(Grounded: extrusion-consistency research pass, 2026-06-18.)*
- Acceleration continuity is layered. Lateral: the fitter keeps κ(s) continuous wherever geometry permits (clothoids / clothoid-spline so κ never steps — including a clothoid half ramping κ from 0 to an arc's curvature at line↔arc seams); any residual κ-step it cannot remove is **not** a fail-loud but a closed-form velocity cap `v ≤ √(a/κ)` at the step — since `a_c = v²κ`, slowing shrinks the acceleration step — graceful degradation to mainline corner-capping. Tangential: a single shared boundary sample node in the joint sweep, jerk handled by the 1D S-curve. The fitter guarantees collinearity (G1) at every segment boundary except move starts. **Jerk ownership: the velocity solver is the safety net (S-curves + speed caps); the fitter is a cost-reducing optimizer that raises corner speed and reduces how often the cap binds — the two compose, neither replaces the other.**
- Through a chain of back-to-back corners the fitter's object is a single **continuous κ(s) profile** (a clothoid spline — piecewise-linear κ), not independently-rounded corners: κ stays continuous and need not return to zero between neighbors, which removes the acceleration discontinuity, avoids needless inter-corner slowdown (faster), and deletes the blend-overlap special case. When room is tight the profile simply stays elevated (smaller / merged clothoids) rather than colliding; only when no within-δ continuous fit exists does the solver's speed cap take over. V1 ships per-corner blends with the safety net; global continuous-κ fitting is a later, measurable fitter upgrade that does not touch the solver.
- Segments are a Rust enum `{ Line | Arc | Clothoid }` plus a follower/virtual-path channel (extruder = follower; pure retraction = zero-length spatial path). Each is closed-form in design space; the clothoid's `κ(s)` is linear/closed-form, and its position uses a Fresnel approximation at execution lowering — no generic numerical integration of κ, no `dyn` dispatch. The velocity planner's read-contract is **κ-space only** (`L`, `κ(0)`, `κ(L)`, `σ=dκ/ds` per segment; `κ⁻`, `κ⁺`, `σ⁻`, `σ⁺`, the G1-flag per junction); position/Fresnel is barred from the planner and lives only at execution lowering. Because κ is closed-form the curvature peak is an **endpoint** (no root-find — this is what dropping G5's cubic-in-s κ buys). "Velocity planning needs only κ" is **phase-qualified**: the jerk-unaware sweep (build step 5) reads only κ; the jerk-aware lookahead (step 6+) additionally reads `σ`. Therefore `σ` lands on the read-contract **from day one** — populated-and-finite but **uncalled** in v1 — so the v1→v2 jerk upgrade is purely additive (a new consumer, no IR→IR seam migration). The one cross-seam fail-loud invariant is **L-consistency**: `σ == (κ(L)−κ(0))/L`, validated at the fitter→planner seam. *(Grounded: segment-IR contract research pass, 2026-06-18.)*
- Machine limits are a swappable convex body, queried only as "max accel/vel in direction d." V1 body: a global scalar XY disk × a separate Z limit (3D from day one). *(Carried from the original map; not re-examined in design discussion.)*
- V1 user knobs: `max_velocity`, absolute `max_accel`, and junction deviation δ — the latter equivalently Square Corner Velocity, `SCV = √(a_max/κ_peak(90°, δ))`, with corner-angle dependence from a closed-form `κ_peak(angle, δ)`. `max_velocity` lives in the machine-limit body and is also settable per-move via the gcode `F` feedrate word; it is what binds top speed where curvature is zero (straights). A volumetric-flow cap `Q_max` (with the slicer's bead `w·h`) is a further ceiling — today slicers enforce it, but the planner caps defensively against the PA-augmented transient.
- **Extrusion consistency is a first-class objective, co-equal with print time** (it is the goal CNC corner-smoothing does not share — they remove material, we lay a controlled bead). Concrete demands the planner must honour: (1) the velocity profile is **C1 through every junction** — a velocity kink is an acceleration impulse, hence a PA-derivative impulse and an uncorrectable flow transient; (2) **no zero-velocity dwell while extruding** (a near-zero corner is the maximal excitation of the PA first-order lag → a blob no PA can cancel, the lag being asymmetric: τ≈37ms building pressure, ≈10ms shedding it); (3) **back-to-back corner dips spaced closer than ~2τ leave the extruder in an unrecoverable transient** — raise the corner speed (clothoid smoothing does this directly) or lengthen the inter-corner segment. This is a second, independent reason for clothoid smoothing beyond speed: it raises the minimum corner velocity and replaces velocity kinks with bounded `dv/dt`. (A sharp zero-velocity corner is *extrusion-wrong*, not merely slow.) **Extrusion makes no demand on the geometry** (not G2, not G3, not κ-continuity): every extrusion-consistency demand lives in the **tangential** channel `s(t)`. PA commands `E_pa = E_nominal + τ·v`, so the extruder acceleration carries `τ·(da/dt) = τ·s⃛` (τ × tangential jerk) — the "post-PA acceleration too violent on the extruder" failure is bounded directly by the S-curve's tangential-jerk limit, not by any curvature primitive. The derived bound is `|τ·ratio·j_tangential| ≤ a_extruder_transient_max` (a machine limit; `FollowerDemand { axis_index, ratio }` is unchanged) — a build-step-6 concern, absent in step 5. *(Grounded: segment-IR contract research pass, 2026-06-18.)*
- The pipeline is a chain of independently testable IR→IR interfaces; the IR is progressively enriched (geometry → +caps → +timing), ideally type-encoded so a pass cannot run before its inputs exist.
- The existing MCU/execution transport and the structured-logging layer are reused unchanged.
- Mainline's gcode "move transforms" are **not** a single front-end coordinate-rewrite here, and need not share one mechanism. Each supported one is handled by the stage and math that fit it: a position-dependent Z field (bed mesh) is a dependent-Z axis the fitter and velocity planner query along the path and the evaluator adds (CAP-5); object cancellation (exclude_object) is move gating at ingest (CAP-7); thermal drift (z_thermal_adjust) is a DC Z bias at the evaluator (CAP-8); a tuning tower is parameter-vs-height modulation at execution (CAP-9, deferred). The planner never rewrites commanded coordinates up front and never splits a move into facets to follow a transform — mainline's `MoveSplitter` (≤0.025 mm-Z facets) is exactly the artifact the fitter exists to remove.
- Build as a walking skeleton: ship the constant-speed-per-feature velocity rule first (mainline-parity), then turn on limit-riding through clothoids as a measurable upgrade — without touching the fitter or the S-curve lookahead.

## Non-goals

- A shaper-aware fitter, or any guarantee that the *post-shaper* path lands within δ. Decided non-goal: the planner never caps acceleration for ringing; δ is a pre-shaper geometry tolerance only. Recoverable later (the fitter is just a pass) without disturbing the architecture.
- Per-axis XY / ellipse constraint body / anisotropic blend shapes and their boundary-discontinuity handling. The convex-body abstraction makes it a later body swap with no downstream changes.
- Native G5 cubic-Bézier input (no slicer emits it). Dropped in V1; a later front-end may add it without refitting.
- Helical (Z-component) G2/G3 arcs. No mainstream slicer emits them; V1 does not support them and fails loudly — pending a decision to instead fold Z as a dependent axis.
- Full jerk-limited, non-convex (SLP) TOPP. Explicitly replaced: lateral jerk into geometry, tangential jerk into a 1D S-curve. Solver-owned jerk (the safety net) does not reintroduce it — the residual caps are precomputed closed-form and fed to the same forward-backward sweep, never co-optimized.
- Per-piece performance tuning to reach ultimate trajectory speed, and whole-print (faster-than-real-time) benchmarking. This spec builds the foundation that *enables* that tuning; it does not perform it.
- Skew correction. An XY affine shear forces a non-uniform (anisotropic) velocity-limit body — junctions optimized against the clean isotropic body would be re-distorted after planning — and turns arcs into ellipses outside the `{Line | Arc | Clothoid}` alphabet. A high-performance machine has no skew; build it out in hardware. Dropped, not deferred.
- Bed tilt as its own feature. A genuinely tilted bed is the planar degenerate of a mesh; modern machines level the gantry physically via `z_tilt` / `quad_gantry_level`, which act at the stepper level below the planner and are unchanged. Not carried as a separate transform.
- Mixing extruder. The multi-input E-channel mixing transform is dropped.

## Success signal

The foundation is sound when every pipeline interface is independently testable and no layer caps the trajectory optimality a downstream piece can reach. Concretely: the walking skeleton runs end-to-end; the heavy convex solver is gone — velocity planning is a forward-backward sweep that keeps up in real time on the active host; and tightening any single pass (a better clothoid, the limit-riding velocity rule, a finer S-curve) improves its output and flows through to the trajectory without edits to adjacent layers.

## Assumptions

- This is a from-scratch rewrite of the geometry and planning layers only; the MCU/execution transport and structured-logging layers stay as-is (confirmed — they work well).
- Whole-print simulation faster than real time does not exist yet, so V1 validation is per-interface and on synthetic or recorded move sequences rather than whole prints.

## Open Questions

- Jerk-limit floor: the largest tangential jerk that still lets `max_accel` reach the no-ring ceiling, set so smoothing never inflates a braking distance past the safety margin (derivable, not guessed).
- `κ_peak(angle, δ)` closed form — **grounded** (research pass 2026-06-18): the road-design shift formula gives `κ_peak ≈ θ²/(24δ)`, accurate to a few % for θ ≲ 60° and a Newton seed converging to the Fresnel-exact value in ~2 steps; the Bertolazzi-Frego `Clothoids` library is a usable reference. Remaining: pin the exact-vs-approx tolerance and the SCV↔δ mapping built on it.
- Forward-backward sweep convergence — **substantially validated** (research pass 2026-06-18): Dong-Stori (2006) *proves* the two-pass bidirectional scan is globally optimal and single-pass for acceleration + curvature (second-order, state-dependent) constraints — exactly our class — and the dangerous TOPP failure mode (zero-inertia / dynamic singularities) is *structurally absent* on a constant-mass Cartesian/CoreXY machine. The closed-form 1D S-curve is likewise non-iterative (Ruckig). Residuals: (a) the optimality gap of *decoupling* lateral and tangential jerk vs a fully-coupled jerk solver — small in principle (jerk costs ~3–5% over jerk-free, Anand 2024) but **unmeasured for FFF**; (b) a G1-but-not-G2 corner slipping past the fitter would make `√(a/κ)` collapse over a sub-segment arc length → the defence is the **node-coverage invariant** (the sweep is node-based, not grid-based: clothoid κ is linear so `κ_peak` is the closed-form endpoint; assert every junction/apex is a node and no κ-step lives mid-segment), not a grid resolution; (c) the single backward pass can be suboptimal on pathological alternating fast/slow runs (Klipper #662) — a window-depth tradeoff to monitor, not an algorithmic flaw.
- Coupling-window bound — **RESOLVED**: bounded by worst-case braking length (see the velocity-planning constraint), not by full stops — normal printing rarely reaches `v=0`. The committed boundary is irreversible (streamed to the MCU). Residual: the safety-factor on the braking estimate — the current planner pads the constant-accel braking time by 10× as a jerk-ramp margin; tighten that once the S-curve ramp math exists, since an oversized window costs real-time and an undersized one corrupts a committed move.
- Ride-the-accel-limit through clothoids (a cheap 1D ODE per clothoid, captures the budget-trading speed) vs the constant-speed-per-feature cap (simpler skeleton): when to switch on.
- Execution lowering: Fresnel approximation degree/tolerance and piece-boundary continuity when baking clothoid + `s(t)` into time-polynomials.
- Global continuous-κ(s) chain fit — **formulation decided, sub-pieces open.** Decided: *no facet-vs-corner classifier* — fit the gentlest κ in the δ-tube around the polyline (in tangent-angle space, deviation ≈ `∫(φ−φ_poly)ds`), restricted to the clothoid-spline family, minimising a convex curvature-rate functional `∫(dκ/ds)²` (= a lateral-jerk-energy proxy) → a banded local QP; "facet vs corner" emerges from the output, never a branch. **Grounded** (research pass 2026-06-18): this functional is the Moreton-Séquin Minimum Variation Curve (1992); in φ-space it is `∫(d²φ/ds²)²` → a natural cubic spline in φ, and the per-corner clothoid blend is its local unconstrained minimiser; the Bertolazzi-Frego `Clothoids` library does O(n) G2 clothoid splines as an implementation vehicle. No FDM firmware does this today — it is genuinely state-of-the-art. Line↔arc seams bridged by a clothoid half (κ ramp `0→κ_arc`, the transition spiral). Open: (a) the exact objective + solver (`∫κ′²` vs `+∫κ²` vs lexicographic min-peak-κ; QP vs geometric sweep) against the real-time budget; (b) the multi-segment δ→κ mapping (coupled generalisation of `κ_peak(θ,δ)`); (c) the fitter lookahead/commit horizon vs the velocity braking horizon (symmetric in principle, constant to pin); (d) the measure-before-build gate — v1 ships per-corner blends; build the global fit only if the measured gap on representative slicer output justifies the QP cost (a local, geometry-only, off-the-velocity-hot-path solver reintroduction — defensible but to be measured, not assumed).
- Back-to-back clothoids with no straight between → a `dκ/ds` step (finite lateral-jerk step, accepted as G2, exactly like a line→clothoid boundary) **as long as κ itself is matched**. Subsumed by the continuous-κ(s) chain fit; the open piece is the fit algorithm above, not whether the step is allowed.
- Fitter-vs-solver jerk ownership — **RESOLVED**: the velocity solver owns jerk as the safety net (tangential S-curve + closed-form speed caps at any residual κ-step), and the fitter is a cost-reducing optimizer, not a hard G2 gate. Geometric blend overlap therefore degrades gracefully (slow the junction) instead of failing loud. The two compose; refining the fitter only reduces how often the cap binds. (See the layered acceleration-continuity constraint.)
- Helical G2/G3 support: fail loudly vs fold Z as a dependent axis. **This is the one place the planar-κ `{Line|Arc|Clothoid}` alphabet can genuinely leak** (a true space curve has torsion, not just curvature) — closing it means decomposing helical G2/G3 into a planar Arc + a linear Z-follower (Z as a follower channel like the extruder), keeping the planner's κ a *plane* curvature. Needs an explicit yes; if anything models a 3D space-curve segment instead, the alphabet does not cover it.
- `{Line|Arc|Clothoid}` alphabet sufficiency vs surface finish (new, 2026-06-18 segment-IR pass): **sufficient** for everything the velocity planner reads (G0–G3 input, closed-form κ) — dropping G5 is causally why (cubic-Bézier was the only input forcing cubic-in-s κ / a root-find). Piecewise-linear κ ≡ the alphabet and is **closed**, but it is the *projection* of the `∫(dκ/ds)²` MVC onto the alphabet (the free minimiser is piecewise-cubic κ = G3); the residual is bounded lateral-jerk *steps* at blend seams. Extrusion is unaffected (it is a tangential-jerk bound in `s(t)`, asks nothing of geometry), so this reduces to a **pure surface-finish** question: measure post-shaper lateral-accel spectral energy at seams in the shaper's inter-notch bands on representative output; below the floor, ship the alphabet; above it — and only then, per the throughput non-negotiable — upgrade to a piecewise-cubic-κ primitive (G3 clothoid-spline / PH-quintic). Does not block V1; an instrument question, not a design commitment.
- Extruder post-IS profile: can the full follower profile computation be simplified? TODO after basic movement works. *(Post-shaper-velocity follower confirmed correct by the 2026-06-18 research pass — the nozzle responds to realized, not commanded, velocity.)*
- Volumetric-flow cap as a planner constraint (new, 2026-06-18): fold `v_flow = Q_max/(w·h)` into the velocity ceiling and guard the PA-augmented transient `(v+τ·a)` against `Q_max`. Open: where `Q_max`, `w`, `h`, and `τ` enter the planner (config vs per-move metadata), and whether the planner trusts the slicer's flow-capping or re-derives it.
- Back-to-back corner spacing vs the PA time constant (new, 2026-06-18): two velocity dips closer than ~2τ leave the extruder in an unrecoverable flow transient. Quantify the minimum corner speed / inter-corner spacing that keeps the transient within PA bandwidth; this is an extrusion-consistency constraint that couples to the coupling-window and the fitter's corner-speed floor.
- Bed-mesh Z and the 2D fitter (new, 2026-06-18): how the 3D velocity cap combines the XY-fitter curvature with the mesh's along-path Z curvature (mesh Hessian) while the fitter itself stays 2D; and whether the mesh `fade(z)` region — which makes the Z field height-dependent — enters the cap or is treated quasi-static within a move.
- Is mesh-Z a follower or a separate field? (new, 2026-06-18) Mesh-Z is settled-nonlinear in XY position, unlike the constant-ratio (arc-length-linear) extruder follower `FollowerDemand`. Open: whether mesh-Z is nevertheless modeled as a *generalized, nonlinear* follower or as a distinct position-dependent Z field — probably the latter, unsettled. Related: on planar machines Z could be a follower of XY (and E a follower of XY), which would not generalize to non-planar motion. Settle before reusing `FollowerDemand` as-is for mesh-Z.
- Mesh interpolant smoothness (new, 2026-06-18): a C1 mesh interpolant steps `d²Z/ds²` at grid-cell boundaries → a residual Z-acceleration step handled by the same closed-form speed cap as a residual κ-step (graceful), vs adopting a C2 interpolant so Z-accel is continuous. Measure before deciding.
- exclude_object resume seam (new, 2026-06-18): confirm that dropping a cancelled object's moves mid-stream introduces no planning discontinuity at the resume seam, and interacts cleanly with the committed-boundary / braking-window invariant.
- *(Carried from the original map, not re-examined in discussion:)* the convex-body shape and the 3D planar pairwise-blend solver.
