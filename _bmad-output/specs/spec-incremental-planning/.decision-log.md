## 2026-06-22 — revision (barrier model replaces compare-and-guard)

**Trigger.** User challenged the differential-comparison framing: "we can know for sure when something is not going to change anymore… if something comes up to cruise velocity, or to 0, everything behind that point is not going to change." Correct, and stronger than the original design.

**Argument accepted.** The only future→past coupling is the velocity backward sweep (forward sweep and fit are append-invariant). Two structural facts — append-only streaming (no geometry inserted between existing moves) and a monotone backward pass (only lowers velocity) — make any seam already at its ceiling unloweravble by a future append. So finality is *detected* (the last saturation barrier scanning back from the frontier), not *estimated-then-compared*.

**Changes.** CAP-2 reframed from "differential equivalence is the contract" to "the commit boundary is a structurally-proven saturation barrier"; the runtime compare-against-full-replan guard is dropped (CAP-3 is now an offline differential *test* plus a fail-loud never-revise assertion). The fixed-time `keep_secs` gate is superseded by the structural barrier (moved to Non-goals). New binding constraint records the trap: the buffer-terminal `v=0` is an artifact (bottom of the trailing ramp, rises on next append), never a barrier — only a ceiling-touch (cruise / curvature cap / real reversal stop) qualifies. Companion replaced: `invalidation-boundary.md` → `finality-barrier.md` (the proof, the trap, the fit leading-edge note, and an input-shaping open question). Two structural facts and the input-shaping coupling are the things the implementation must preserve; they are now explicit in the constraints and the companion.

**Preservation recheck.** All prior source claims still land; the new model tightens rather than drops. New open question added (where input shaping is applied) because it is the one remaining bounded coupling whose presence changes the locked boundary by a fixed margin.

## 2026-06-22 — revision 2 (deferred brake-to-rest)

**Trigger.** User: leave the deceleration time uncommitted and only materialize it when nothing more arrives and it must be submitted — recomputing the brake every batch when only the final move actually needs it is wasteful. Raised the matching worry: a forced submit could find the ramp unsolved.

**Accepted and sharpened.** Added the forward-invariance half of the proof: accelerations (pinned by the past) and brakes into already-buffered corners are also final, so the *only* non-final region is the brake-to-rest bound by the buffer's terminal fiction. Consequence recorded as a constraint: the locked solve terminates at the last barrier's ceiling velocity and never needs the brake-to-rest to commit the prefix. The brake-to-rest becomes flush-only (new CAP-3): materialized at true end-of-stream or at a producer-stall low-watermark triggered with braking+solve+margin of locked lead, which closes the user's "unsolved when it's time" vulnerability — the solve always completes before its first piece dispatches, and a move arriving in the window discards the provisional brake. Over a healthy print the deceleration solve runs once, at the end.

**Changes.** CAP-1 now terminates the locked solve at the barrier ceiling; CAP-2 broadened to the full finality set (forward/ceiling/buffered-corner); CAP-3 is the deferred brake-to-rest with the watermark; old never-revise guard folded into the fail-loud constraint, which also now covers a brake that cannot fit the remaining lead. New open question: what sets the watermark margin (fixed time vs braking-distance-derived + solve budget).

**Preservation recheck.** Intact; the 217 ms-spike root cause is now addressed by *not building* the throwaway tail rather than building it cheaper — a strict improvement that still preserves output-equivalence (the differential test remains the faithfulness check).

## 2026-06-22 — revision 3 (shaping resolved, sweep-reconvergence, margin)

**Inputs from user.** (1) Input shaping and pressure advance are post-solver per-axis post-processors (in sota-motion, not this branch — keep separate), applied to already-planned motion, no effect on limits. (2) For the watermark, derive braking time from max feedrate / current velocity, accel and jerk; keep arcs and clothoids in mind; this is a solver concern, not the fitter.

**Resolutions.** Open Question #1 (shaping) closed: no backward coupling, barrier exact, no setback — recorded as an Assumption with the caveat that moving shaping into the solver would break exactness. Margin clarified by separating two quantities that looked alike: (a) *barrier placement* is done by the backward sweep's reconvergence with the forward/ceiling profile — exact, geometry-aware (arcs/clothoids only shorten the open tail), and crucially O(open tail) because the sweep stops at reconvergence rather than scanning the buffer (this is the real mechanism behind CAP-1's flat-in-depth cost); (b) the jerk-limited braking closed form (`v/a + a/j`, or `2√(v/j)`) is used only to *size the flush-trigger watermark* and bound how many moves stay open, with `v_barrier` (free, tight) for the trigger and section max feedrate as the conservative bound. Confirmed the backward coupling is the velocity solver's alone; the fitter increments separately (δ-local + head-restore).

**Changes.** CAP-1 now names the reconvergence-terminated backward sweep and bounds cost by open-tail length. New constraints: backward-coupling-is-solver-only (with fitter scoping); barrier-respects-real-geometry (with the braking closed form scoped to sizing only). Shaping moved from Open Question to Assumption. Remaining open questions narrowed to the watermark solve-time budget term and the differential-test tolerance. Companion gains a reconvergence-termination section and a "closed form is for sizing, not locating" section.

**Preservation recheck.** Intact and tightened; no source claim dropped.
