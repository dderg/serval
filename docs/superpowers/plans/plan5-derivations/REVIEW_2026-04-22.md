# Adversarial review — Plan 5 "Direct-quintic + Pillar 1" design spec

**Reviewer:** opus adversarial subagent
**Date:** 2026-04-22
**Target:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-04-22-plan5-direct-quintic-pillar1-design.md`
**Companion artifacts reviewed:** `direct_quintic_architecture.md`, `fir_companion_kernel.md`, `new_shaper_family.md`, `saturation_feedback.md`, `lookahead_extension_audit.md`

## Summary

The Plan 5 spec is ambitious and the research that underpins it is mostly sound — the cardinal B-spline choice is well-motivated, the invertibility argument for the new family is correct, the saturation-feedback derivation survives scrutiny, and the tagged-union architecture choice is defensible. The overall direction is right.

But the spec itself has load-bearing integrity problems that would propagate into implementation. The D1 A_axis table is numerically wrong in a way that is inconsistent with its own research artifact; literature citations have wrong volume numbers, wrong years, and at least one citation that does not appear to exist; the scope claim undersells the kinematics-file blast radius by ~half; and the out-of-scope claim on the extruder contradicts what `kin_extruder.c` actually does. None of these problems is fatal to the architecture, but each is exactly the kind of "small" detail that eats a weekend of implementation time when discovered at the C-side refactor stage.

The 3-4 week effort estimate is optimistic given the under-scoped kinematics fanout, the unaddressed `struct smoother` layout redesign, and the research-to-spec numerical drift that suggests the D1 tables have not been numerically validated end-to-end. My verdict is **major revision needed** before implementation — the numerical errors in the load-bearing tables must be fixed, literature citations corrected, scope expanded to cover all kinematics actually touched, and the extruder-out-of-scope argument re-examined in light of the shaper pipeline inside `kin_extruder.c`.

## Found issues

### Critical (must-fix before implementation)

**C1. D1 A_axis table is internally inconsistent with its own research artifact.**

Spec `specs/2026-04-22-plan5-direct-quintic-pillar1-design.md:225-231` gives:

| variant | spec σ²_T / T_sm² | spec A_axis | research artifact A_axis | correct theoretical σ²_T/T_sm² |
|--|--|--|--|--|
| bs1 | 0.1667 | 3964 | 3810 | 0.04167 |
| bs2 | 0.1389 | 3810 | 3650 | 0.02778 |
| bs3 | 0.1250 | 3730 | 3635 | 0.02083 |
| bs4 | 0.1167 | 3665 | 3668 | 0.01667 |
| bs5 | 0.1111 | 3635 | 3723 | 0.01389 |

Both columns are wrong. For a cardinal B-spline of order m, the exact second central moment is σ²_B = (m+1)/12 (in canonical time units), giving σ²_T/T_sm² = 1/(12(m+1)) — this is what `new_shaper_family.md:554-556` and the Unser 1993 reference both confirm. The spec's σ²_T/T_sm² column is 4× too large and has the wrong monotonicity pattern (spec decreases by ~0.83 per step; correct values decrease by 0.67 per step).

Meanwhile the A_axis column in the spec is monotone decreasing, but the research artifact's correctly-computed A_axis is **non-monotone with minimum at bs3** (3810 → 3650 → 3635 → 3668 → 3723). The non-monotonicity is physically correct: T_sm grows ~√(m+1) per the design target, so σ²_T = T_sm²/(12(m+1)) ≈ const as m varies, and the small residual variation has a minimum mid-family.

**The spec's table is fabricated and contradicts the derivation in `new_shaper_family.md:667-680`.** This is the load-bearing numerical claim that drives the migration-table recommendations (D6) and the risk 4 discussion. Fix it or remove the table.

**C2. Literature citations have wrong year/volume/pages.**

- Besset & Béarée: spec cites CEP 67:157-167, actual is CEP **66:169-180** (see `plan5-derivations/fir_companion_kernel.md` and `new_shaper_family.md` both list 67:157-167 — same wrong citation propagated).
- Biagiotti & Melchiorri: spec cites "2012", correct year is **2008** (Springer, ISBN 978-3-540-85628-3). This error appears in the spec and all four research artifacts.
- Wang & Altintas 2022 CIRP Annals and Altintas et al. 2023 CIRP-JMST references — **could not be verified**. Wang & Altintas in that specific form did not surface in web search; Ever-Hanley is not a recognizable co-author of Altintas. These are the only two citations anchoring the D4 saturation-feedback argument to "prior art." Without them, the argument stands on the math alone, which is fine, but the spec's claim of literature support (line 684 "Wang & Altintas (2022-2023), CIRP Annals / CIRP-JMST. Model-predictive feedforward under actuator saturation") should be removed or replaced with verifiable references.

**C3. Out-of-scope claim on extruder axis contradicts `kin_extruder.c`.**

Spec `specs/...:144-148`: "Extruder-axis feedforward inverse — Extruder follows the physical path via pressure-advance, not the stepper-commanded position; Pillar 1 is XY only."

`klippy/chelper/kin_extruder.c:184-226` (`extruder_calc_position`) shows the extruder stepper IS commanded through a shaper (it calls `shaper_calc_position` and `pa_range_integrate` on the XY axes to compute its position). Per `klippy/extras/input_shaper.py:417-431`, the extruder's smoother comes from the same `INPUT_SMOOTHERS` table that Plan 5 is replacing. After D1 lands, the extruder's smoother will be a `bs*` variant too.

Therefore: either (a) the extruder gets its own feedforward inverse (not currently in scope), which means the extruder's shaped position command no longer matches the XY command the printer is tracing, **or** (b) the extruder continues to smooth without inversion, and the XY vs E axes now have different effective group delays, which will break PA sync.

The `lookahead_extension_audit.md §6.8` actually notices this issue in passing ("If Plan 5 also adds an extruder inverse, that inverse's T_h adds on top") but concludes the extruder doesn't need an inverse "because extruder follows physical, not commanded" — an argument that conflates the PA conversion (physical-path-dependent) with the XY-axis shaper convolution that still runs on the extruder stepper path. The spec inherits the flawed conclusion.

This needs a proper derivation before D3 can be claimed complete. Either the extruder gets an inverse (D3 expands), or the argument for "XY only" needs to be rigorously stated with a sync-error bound.

**C4. Scope undersells kinematics fanout.**

Spec `specs/...:156-158` lists "polar / rotary_delta" as "the only kinematics files not touched". In `klippy/chelper/` there are actually 8 kin files that call `move_get_coord` / `move_get_distance`:

`kin_cartesian.c`, `kin_corexy.c`, `kin_corexz.c`, `kin_delta.c`, `kin_deltesian.c`, `kin_idex.c`, `kin_polar.c`, `kin_rotary_delta.c`, plus `kin_winch.c` and `kin_extruder.c`.

Spec's D2b `specs/...:311-315` claims "Cartesian, CoreXY, delta are required for Plan 5; polar / rotary_delta flagged for follow-up" — but that leaves **corexz, deltesian, idex, winch** unaccounted for. Idex (for IDEX printers — real user base in the Kalico community) is not a minor case. All four need explicit handling, either in scope or with a clear deferral + printer-load-time error.

### Important (should-fix before implementation)

**I1. `struct smoother` piecewise extension is hand-waved.**

Spec `specs/...:183-192` says "extend `struct smoother` to hold an array of piece descriptors" — but the current struct (`klippy/chelper/integrate.h:8-13`) holds flat `c0[12], c1[12], c2[12]` arrays for a single polynomial of degree ≤11 and the FFI signature `input_shaper_set_smoother_params(sk, axis, n, a[], t_sm)` takes a single flat coefficient array. Extending to 6 pieces × degree-5 polynomials changes:

- Struct layout (C ABI)
- FFI signature (Python-C boundary)
- `calc_antiderivatives` (piece-indexed integration)
- `init_smoother` (piece-wise normalization, each piece computed separately)
- `range_integrate` (kin_shaper.c:105-160) — must correctly handle queries spanning piece boundaries
- `AxisInputSmoother` in `klippy/extras/input_shaper.py:417-431` — must marshal piece data to C

`new_shaper_family.md §9.5` acknowledges this as "not zero work" but the spec hides the complexity. This is at least a week of careful C refactoring with heavy numerical regression testing to ensure the piecewise integration preserves the byte-identical behavior the existing forward path has. The effort estimate (D1 = 3-4 days) is optimistic.

**I2. Smoother moment count claim: "6 moments" may be a degree error.**

Spec `specs/...:261-267` says "rewrite `smoother_antiderivatives` from 3 moments to 6" because "a quintic `x(t) = Σ c_k t^k` (k=0..5) ... requires six moments `(m0..m5)`". Let me verify from the current code.

`klippy/chelper/integrate.c:51-63` does indeed compute `base · it0 − start_v · it1 + half_accel · it2` — three moments supporting a degree-2 polynomial in t (position = p₀ + v₀t + ½at²). For a degree-5 polynomial path `x(t) = c₀ + c₁t + c₂t² + c₃t³ + c₄t⁴ + c₅t⁵`, the integral `∫ w(τ) x(t-τ) dτ` yields six terms, each a moment `∫ τᵏ w(τ) dτ` for k ∈ {0,1,2,3,4,5}. So **6 moments is arithmetically correct for degree 5**. Verified.

However, the spec doesn't address the numerical conditioning of the moment arithmetic at degree 5. `t^5 ≈ 1e-10` at T_sm ~ 40 ms, and after normalization by T_sm⁵ the inner products may have catastrophic cancellation on the `h - hst` vs `-hst` subtraction. `direct_quintic_architecture.md §4` flags this as "unverified." The spec's D2a regression gate ("bit-identical outputs on a linear move") doesn't catch this because linear moves have c₃=c₄=c₅=0 — the new code paths are never exercised.

**I3. A_axis formula may not directly apply to piecewise kernels.**

The spec (and `new_shaper_family.md §6`) asserts `A_axis = 2·target_smoothing/σ²_T`, inherited from `plan4-derivations/A_axis_smooth_is.md`. The derivation in that document (I didn't open it for this review) is presumably for single-polynomial smoothers. For piecewise-polynomial kernels, the argument still goes through because `σ²_T` is a global second-moment property of the kernel, not piece-by-piece — but the spec doesn't explicitly connect the dots. Flag a one-paragraph proof obligation for D1.

**I4. `G = ‖h‖₁ < 2` claim requires caveat.**

Spec `specs/...:208-212` claims worst-case G = 2.00 at `pb_max = 0.3 f_sh`. Research artifact `new_shaper_family.md:230-234` confirms this numerically (G values 1.93, 1.92, 2.00, 1.99, 1.95). At `pb_max = 0.5 f_sh` the worst case jumps to 2.84 (bs4). Fine.

But the L¹ norm is computed via numerical IFFT of a tapered frequency-domain inverse. The truncation to 77-136 ms and the 5% Tukey window are design choices that affect G. Changing any of them (e.g. shorter T_h for runtime cost reasons) changes G. The spec should publish G per variant as derived numerical constants with their exact design parameters, not quote them as kernel properties — they are design-parameter-dependent numerical outputs.

**I5. `v_jerk` cap in `v_cap_fn` is not updated in D4.**

`klippy/blendquintic.py:584-586` currently uses `limits.jerk_max` directly. The saturation-feedback argument in `saturation_feedback.md §2.5, §5.3` claims the existing `v_jerk` cap will handle the tangential jerk concern automatically "as long as A_axis is passed through with the inverse-corrected value." But that requires `A_axis` itself to be set to `a_max / G_worst`, not just the centripetal cap. The spec D4 says `a_eff = a_max / G_worst` is applied in `v_cent`, but doesn't specify the same adjustment for `j_eff` (which flows through `compute_shaper_bounds`'s `A_axis` into rotation-jerk calculation). Clarify.

**I6. Fused kernel width claim: 3×T_sm vs stated 2.5×.**

Spec `specs/...:218-219`: "`T_sm + T_h = 3 · T_sm`" assumes T_h = 2·T_sm. Research artifact `new_shaper_family.md §8.2` agrees (T_h/T_sm = 2). But `fir_companion_kernel.md §6.3` originally assumed T_h/T_sm ≈ 1.5 ("fused T ≈ 2.5 T_sm"). The new research supersedes that — spec's 3×T_sm is consistent with the final design. OK, just a clarification note.

**I7. `target_smoothing = 0` sentinel survival is not rigorously exercised.**

Spec `specs/...:641-644` lists this as risk 8 with mitigation "regression test: `target_smoothing = 0` under any `bs*` still disables the cap." But the current code disables the cap by `_extract_shapers` returning `[]` (per the memory note). Does that path still work when `AxisShaperSnapshot` has gained an `inverse_G` field and the v_cap now multiplies by G? Verify: if `limits.shapers = []`, the `G_worst = 1.0` loop is skipped and `a_eff = a_max` — the cap reduces to the existing Plan 4 form. OK, **this specific claim survives**. Good.

### Minor (worth noting, not blocking)

**M1. "EI-family" nomenclature is inconsistent.**

Spec `specs/...:500-506` migration table and `fir_companion_kernel.md §2.1` refer to "EI-family" kernels including `smooth_mzv`, `smooth_ei`, etc. — but `smooth_mzv` and `smooth_zv` are not EI-style notch kernels; they are polynomial approximations to ZV/MZV impulse trains. The claim "every EI-family kernel has a first spectral null below f_sh" conflates optimization intent (suppress at resonance) with notch placement. Verified from `klippy/extras/shaper_defs.py:70-90`: these are hand-optimized polynomials, not structural notch filters. The claim should say "5 of 6 smoothers have spectral zeros in (0, f_sh)", and acknowledge the cause is polynomial optimization for V(ω) ≤ 5%, which naturally places zeros — not "EI-family" structure per se.

**M2. Classical impulse shaper list in out-of-scope is wrong.**

Spec `specs/...:141-142` says "Classic FIR shapers (`zv`, `mzv`, `ei`, `zvd`, `2hump_ei`, `3hump_ei`). These stay unchanged." But `klippy/extras/shaper_defs.py:208-211` shows the fork already removed `ei`, `zvd`, `2hump_ei`, `3hump_ei` from `INPUT_SHAPERS`. Only `zv` and `mzv` are there as impulse shapers. The spec should either reference only what's actually in-tree, or confirm that the other names still go through some other path.

**M3. Risk 3 CPU cost: "triples" is right but misleading.**

Spec `specs/...:588-591`: "Current `smoother_calc_position` visits ~200-400 samples per call; post-Plan-5 ~600-1200." This is the sample-count proxy. But per-sample cost is not constant: the new piecewise kernel requires a piece-index lookup per sample (6-way branch for bs5), which is warmer in the branch predictor but adds ~2-3 ns per sample. Also the degree-5 Horner is more FLOPs than the current setup. Realistic cost multiplier is likely **3.5-4×**, not 3×. Trident sysload at `~45-70k toolhead accel` is already 1.2-1.5, so 4× smoother cost has the potential to push over the ceiling. Profile before shipping (spec says this under risk mitigation — acknowledged).

**M4. `motion_report` schema change is risky.**

Spec `specs/...:578-586` proposes `version: 2` field on the websocket payload. This is the right approach, but will silently break any Mainsail/Fluidd/Moonraker consumer that doesn't version-check. Frontend projects should be told about the change before it lands, not after. Add an announcement task to D6.

**M5. `klipper-sim` break is deferred as non-blocker.**

Spec `specs/...:351-353`: "track as a follow-up task, not a blocker for Plan 5." Per memory note `reference_klipper_sim.md`, klipper-sim has 59 tests and is used for offline planner validation. Breaking it means losing that validation path for the most invasive Plan 5 change. Prefer: fix klipper-sim in the same commit batch as D2b.

2. **"Altintas, Ever-Hanley, Erkorkmaz (2023)"** (`saturation_feedback.md §7`). "Ever-Hanley" is not a discoverable author. Likely fabricated.

5. **"worst-case G: 2.00"** at pb_max = 0.3·f_sh (spec line 208). True per numerical experiment, but only under the specific `T_h = 2·T_sm` + Tukey α=0.05 + cosine-taper transition choices. Publish G as a design-parameter-dependent output, not a kernel property.
6. **"Effort estimate: 3-4 weeks"** (spec line 540). ## Math verified

1. **Cardinal B-spline Fourier transform = `sinc^{m+1}(ω T_1/2π)`** — verified from Unser 1993 reference and standard signal-processing textbooks. The (m+1)-fold self-convolution of a unit-rectangle yields this spectrum.
2. **First spectral zero at `(m+1)/T_sm`** — verified from the table at `specs/...:225-231` — all five variants have first zero ≥ 1.29·f_sh at f_sh = 40 Hz, outside the shaper passband.
3. **σ²_T = T_sm²/(12·(m+1))** — verified symbolically and cross-checked against `new_shaper_family.md §10`'s numerical integration in `verify_all()`. This is the correct formula; **the spec's D1 table does NOT use this formula**.
4. **L¹-L∞ inequality `|h ⊛ ẍ|∞ ≤ ‖h‖₁ · ‖ẍ‖∞`** — standard real-analysis result. Verified.
5. **v_sat derivation: `v_sat = sqrt(a_max/(G·κ))`** — follows from (L¹-L∞ bound) + (centripetal peak binds) + (v² ≤ a/(G·κ)). Verified.
6. **6 antiderivative moments needed for degree-5 polynomial path** — verified by direct expansion: `∫ w(τ) (c₀+c₁τ+c₂τ²+c₃τ³+c₄τ⁴+c₅τ⁵) dτ` yields 6 scalar products. Correct.

## Architecture concerns

- **Tagged union in `struct move`** is a defensible choice per the trade-off analysis in `direct_quintic_architecture.md §2-3`. The `MOVE_LINEAR` vs `MOVE_QUINTIC` branch is small and hot-path-friendly.
- **But: the spec claim that `move_get_coord` "dispatches on `m->kind`"** needs a careful look at the inlining discipline — the current `move_get_coord` is inline in `trapq.c:31-39` (per `direct_quintic_architecture.md §2.1`); adding a branch either (a) keeps it inline and every kin_*.c re-inlines the branch 10 times, bloating I-cache, or (b) de-inlines it, adding a function-call cost per step-gen query. The spec doesn't address this choice. Benchmark before committing.
- **`range_integrate` on piecewise kernel crossing a piece boundary** is non-trivial. The current code handles move boundaries; now we also have kernel-piece boundaries inside a single move's contribution. The spec claims "piece-index lookup per sample" works without elaborating. I'd want to see the pseudocode for the `range_integrate` → `calc_antiderivatives` interaction before committing.
- **ZPETC claim** in `new_shaper_family.md §4.2` — the doc says "minimum phase with no allpass component" trivially factors, so ZPETC reduces to 1/W directly. This is true, but the "infinite support" conclusion means the design falls back to truncation + windowing, i.e. what `fir_companion_kernel.md` already did. Don't oversell the ZPETC path as a real alternative.

## Verdict

**Major revision needed.** The architecture is sound, the family choice is well-motivated, and the saturation-feedback math is correct. But the D1 tables are numerically inconsistent with their own derivation artifact, two of five literature citations are wrong on year/volume/pages and two more appear to be fabricated, the extruder out-of-scope argument is shaky, and the kinematics scope is under-specified by about half.

Required before implementation kickoff:
1. Fix the D1 A_axis + σ²_T table (replace with research-artifact numbers) and re-derive the migration table in D6 from corrected A_axis values.
2. Correct all literature citations (Besset-Béarée 2017 → vol. 66, pp. 169-180; Biagiotti-Melchiorri → 2008). Remove or replace unverifiable Wang-Altintas / Altintas-Ever-Hanley-Erkorkmaz references.
3. Re-examine extruder path: either derive why the extruder's XY-smoother path doesn't need an inverse for coherent Pillar 1 operation, or expand D3 scope to include it.
4. Expand D2b scope to cover `kin_corexz.c`, `kin_deltesian.c`, `kin_idex.c`, `kin_winch.c` — or explicitly document each as unsupported at printer-load time.
5. Revise effort estimate to 5-7 weeks given the above.
6. Add a `struct smoother` layout redesign sub-section to D1, with FFI signature change and numerical conditioning validation for degree-5 moment arithmetic.

Once those land, the plan is shippable. The research is largely good; the spec just needs to catch up to it numerically and editorially.
